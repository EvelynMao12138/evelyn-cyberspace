Secret of Shame

I Left Him. Why Was I Still Calling?

By Evelyn Mao

I have a secret that is too shameful to share: I’m still keeping in touch with my abusive ex-boyfriend, who hit me multiple times, degraded me, and manipulated me.

My best friends always said I should leave him as soon as possible. They told me I was so swept up in him because I still seem indecisive after the violence, even though I tried my best to break up with him, physically stay away from him, meanwhile trying to pretend nothing had happened in school, and still struggle to get all As in every class. This summer, I was diagnosed with PTSD and depression, yet people always joke, “You can find someone better at Harvard”, when I tried to complain about my situation. It’s obvious that I can’t walk out of the trauma that quickly, and I’m not that casual in relationships. Over time, I realized very few of my friends can fully resonate with my experience, because most of them are single and men-hating, just like how I was when I was single. Sometimes I feel that some of them view me as a joke, because before our break up, my every conversation with them includes that men and I will bring my boyfriend to girls’ dates. I tried to make everyone like him, now, it seems like I’m trying to let everyone know how awful he is.

Though few people heard out my dilemma and treated it seriously, I don’t blame them for that. I always have a feeling that it’s mostly my fault for having a bad taste in men, who is bald, full of acne, and abusive. After break up, everything that bothered me about him started to fit together in my mind, and I was like Sherlock Holmes, saying: Damn, I should have notice all the clues earlier. I’m so cooked and have lost too much in this relationship at the point that I feel nobody can understand me, and I get tired of re-explaining everything from the beginning. I feel so alone in the middle of the night, but when I want to call someone that I can talk to, I don’t even know whether it should be my ex or my best friends. “ Hi, babe.” That’s what happens next.

I tried to talk to a therapist who speaks Mandarin in Boston. It took me several months to schedule a phone call with CAHMS and another few weeks to get referred to her. We had our first meeting online. She asked me what happened on the night he hit me; strangely enough, I can’t remember anything. I can’t even explain how it happened from beginning to end, in a logical order and in my mother tongue; same with a lot of my other traumas. “ Your brain is trying very hard to protect you,” She said, “ And you did very well leaving him, and you did everything you can.” Then I told her that I still call my ex daily; the atmosphere became subtle.

In addition, ever since I broke up, I feel ashamed when I recall the things we did together in the heat of the moment, which are absolute secrets I will take to my grave. Born into a Chinese family, my parents are all conservative and old-fashioned, obviously a “no sex before marriage” type. However, in my 3rd year of college, I moved in with my ex in Irvine without telling my parents. This is when everything started getting further and further off track after I re-analyzed what’s wrong with my relationship. I behaved like a spy daily. I pretended I lived alone using AI-generated pictures of my apartment and still video chatted daily with my mom every night in bed, when my ex was in the living room. At the same time, he shared this news with his parents, but he got no “keep safe” or “is she reliable” from his parents, but something more like “congrats” and “my son has grown up”.

At first, we cooked together, and he always drove me to Costco. A few weeks later, he no longer helped me cook. Instead, he was playing video games every time I wanted a hand from him. He also rewrote the rules: whoever cooked the meal should also wash the dishes. Therefore, every day after a long day of school, I had to be his mom, making dinner for two and washing all the dishes. Meanwhile, I was a feminist and a woman leader in school. My favourite lyric was “ I don’t cook, I don’t clean, but let me tell you how I got this ring,” but now I had to do both. Such a shame.

In addition to housework, I’m also sexually exploited. After he had finished a meal, I was already exhausted due to the housework, but he demanded more. He turned from “can you help me” to “ I’ll be disappointed or angry if you don’t help me”. Sometimes I wondered why he wanted a blow job from me if a silicone doll could do the exact same thing, but I pleased him, even though I never really wanted to. Such a shame.

Many will ask if my relationship with him is so unequal and shameful, why I still have contact with him. I would say that I’ve just shared what went wrong, but it’s only part of our story and relationship. During the 3 years, I have also been through and taken away a lot of the good things. It just feels that, as a woman, the price for making mistakes is higher in a relationship, and we are easier to be exploited. PTSD brings me memory loss, freezing in conflicts, and language barriers, while depression brings me sleep deprivation and brain fog, but I felt I sacrificed more for the things I did for him every day. In addition to not being understood by friends, the sunk cost effect is also a main factor. I’m not a masochist, but I don’t have the energy to date someone new and start everything from the beginning again. Now that I already know the ending, I won’t have expectations of him or worry about our future. I’m convincing myself that I’m playing with him and there are no real emotions involved every time I call him.

As I’m writing this, I have already forgotten a lot of things as I tried to recall trauma too many times. The more I replay trauma in my mind, the more unreal it feels. Sometimes I can feel that I can travel through time and space to fix our relationship. I know I’m deceiving myself, but what else can I do?

Author’s reflection

Secret of Shame is a personal essay about the main character being ashamed of her relationship with her abusive ex-boyfriend, which caused her PTSD and depression. The narrative is inspired by the movie Life of Pi, and the story is based on my own experience. My favourite line of the movie is “Which is the better story, the story with animals or the story without animals?” In that movie, the main character lied that he was on a boat with a tiger, but instead the tiger represented his survival instinct and himself eating other survivors on the boat. This is the way he deals with trauma: he reimagines the story again and again, tells everyone a lie, and even eventually deceives himself. In the personal essay, the narrator also has to deal with her trauma again and again while narrating her story in a logical order, so there are contradictions in her thoughts, deception, and indecisiveness.

The personal essay explores why the narrator feels ashamed about her secret. In the beginning, she starts from feeling ashamed for still keeping in touch with her ex. This hook triggers the reader’s voyeuristic curiosity to know the secret and why it’s shameful. According to the Week 4 class notes: situation vs. story, this is the situation, and the readers need to figure out what actually happened to her later in the story.

Starting from the second paragraph, the story embraces the dual-self narration. The narrator compares herself to Sherlock Holmes after looking back at herself. Her perception of the world might be magnified in her narration due to her sensitivity: she thinks people don’t understand her trauma and never treated her trauma seriously. She becomes self-blaming and feels that she’s a joke. In paragraph three, the story explores choices under pressure as the narrator needs to choose between an abusive ex, whom she lived with for 2 years, and her best friends, who don’t really know how to respond to her trauma. Readers might start to understand the narrator’s choice to call her ex in the middle of the night, and in the later paragraphs, their minds might shift again because of how she was exploited.

Starting from paragraph five, the story reveals what really bothers the narrator and makes her feel ashamed. The following paragraphs explore the topic of power and positionality in intimate relationships. I chose the apartment as the setting as it’s a living place they shared and a place that their power gets divided and is the place that she can’t escape. The narrator becomes the caregiver and pleaser in daily life. The repetition of “ such a shame” highlights that it’s a painful past that she cannot bring herself to talk about or share with someone else: She is successful in her life and is also a feminist, but on the contrary, what she did in the apartment is being coerced by the unhealthy relationship. She puts herself in a wife’s position, yet they are just in a romantic relationship. In fact, when looking at the entire story, the narrator didn’t mention anything about direct physical abuse in a graphic way, which is the most important cause of her breakup, implying her escapist self when dealing with trauma and her narrative omission.

The use of retrospective narration is the main technique in this personal essay. The narrator’s rational self-reflection and self-blame conflict with her emotional decision. In the last two paragraphs, she started to defend her relationship by pointing out that the readers don’t know everything, and she only showed the bad side of her ex, which might make the readers stand on the same side as her friends. That is exactly how in the beginning the narrator describe what her friends behaved and the reason why she felt isolated. Reader’s stance shift back and forward throughout the reading is a part of the reading experience. Whether they can empathize with the narrator depends on their life experience and values and there’s no right and wrongs of the answer.

The ending further highlights her PTSD and depression symptoms, adding more unreliability and uncertainty to her narration. She also might have experienced dissociation due to trauma. The question, “What else can I do?”, leaves an open-ended question for readers and the tension unsolved in the end. It invites readers to provide their suggestions and point of view, though the narrator might already have an answer in her mind.

Throughout the reading, the narrator seems like she is making an emotional decision with great rationality. The sense of disconnect in “I know our ending, and that’s why I still choose him” invites readers to consider two deeper questions: Does a love story have to end happily? Can they still go back to the way they were after the trauma she experienced? She gets a little mad at her friend and therapist's stance change from her complaint about her ex being an abusive partner to the fact that she still contacts him, because she was looking for someone who agrees with her no matter what she says, but most of the time, people will react like, “What do you want me to do after I’ve complained about your ex with you for hours?”

The difference between this personal essay and Life of Pi, is this narrator is the zebra who witnessed herself being eaten up by the tiger. She has already forgotten the most graphic scene of violence, but she remembers the daily erosion of her sense of self: cooking, washing dishes, and pleasing her boyfriend after a long day of work, which are small matters that become the main resource when narrating this relationship.

Lastly, going back to the definition of secret of shame mentioned in the beginning and what’s expanded in later contexts, I wanted readers to reconsider what the narrator wanted to hide and what is actually so shameful that she doesn’t want to share; why and what caused their stance shift throughout the reading, because it constructs how they react to their own moral decisions.
